concept: |
  The brief was unusually short. The family wanted a house that felt calm on a
  weekday evening, not one that performed for guests on a Saturday night.

  We worked outward from the light. The villa faces east across an open plot, so
  the plan holds the living volume open to the morning and pulls the private
  rooms back into shade. Every surface was then chosen for how it behaves in
  that light rather than how it photographs under studio lamps.

  Restraint did most of the work. A limited palette of three materials, repeated
  at different scales, lets the proportions of the rooms carry the interest.
execution: |
  Executed as a single turnkey contract over eleven months. Our team held
  sequencing across stone, joinery, metalwork and lighting so that no trade
  worked against another, and the joinery was dry-fitted off site before
  anything reached the house.

  The double-height window wall required the travertine to be book-matched
  across four storeys of elevation. Slabs were selected at source and numbered
  before shipping so the vein continued uninterrupted through the stair void.
materials:
  - Italian travertine, honed and book-matched across the full stair elevation
  - European oak veneer, rift-sawn, in a single flitch throughout the ground floor
  - Blackened steel joinery with hand-finished brass detailing
  - Architectural linear lighting integrated into the ceiling structure
  - Natural wool and linen upholstery in undyed tones
highlights:
  - Book-matched travertine continuing across four storeys of stair elevation
  - Single-flitch oak veneer across every ground-floor joinery run
  - Concealed architectural lighting with no visible fittings in the main volume
  - Eleven-month turnkey delivery under one accountable team
