concept: |
  The brief was unusually short. The family wanted a house that felt calm on a
  weekday evening, not one that performed for guests on a Saturday night.

  We worked outward from the light. The main volume opens west across the
  valley, so the plan holds the living and dining together against the glazing
  and pulls the private rooms back into shade. Every surface was then chosen for
  how it behaves in that light rather than how it photographs under studio
  lamps.

  Restraint did the rest. A limited palette repeated at different scales lets the
  proportion of the volume carry the interest, and the lighting is layered so the
  room can be bright, quiet or nearly dark without moving a single fitting.
execution: |
  Executed as a single turnkey contract over eleven months. Our team held
  sequencing across stone, joinery, metalwork and lighting so that no trade
  worked against another, and the joinery was dry-fitted off site before
  anything reached the house.

  The feature elevation required the marble to be book-matched across the full
  height of the volume. Slabs were selected at source and numbered before
  shipping, so the vein continues uninterrupted from the console line to the
  ceiling reveal.
materials:
  - Book-matched marble, honed, across the full-height feature elevation
  - Fluted walnut panelling with concealed vertical lighting
  - Antique bronze ring pendants, made to a bespoke diameter
  - Large-format polished stone flooring laid to a continuous grid
  - Undyed wool rugs and linen upholstery in a single tonal family
highlights:

  - Living, dining and bar resolved as one continuous space
