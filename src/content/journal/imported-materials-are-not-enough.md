Sourcing is the part of our work clients ask about most, and it is the part that
matters least on its own.

## Three capabilities, usually bought separately

A finished interior depends on three things happening in agreement.

- **Design** decides where a material goes and at what scale it is read.
- **Sourcing** secures the specific piece the design assumed, not an equivalent.
- **Execution** installs it in an order that makes the design possible.

Most projects procure these from three parties who never meet. The design is
drawn without knowing what is available, the material is bought on price against
a written specification, and the site resolves the difference between them with
whatever is to hand.

## Where it goes wrong

The failure is rarely dramatic. It is a vein direction that runs the wrong way
across a joint. A slab that was ordered by name rather than selected in person,
so the tonal range across the wall is wider than the drawing assumed. A brass
reveal cut to fit after the stone was set, instead of installed before it.

Each of these is small. Together they are the entire difference between a
material that looks expensive and one that looks bought.

> A quarry can sell anyone the same stone. What it cannot sell is the decision
> about which slab, in which order, against which drawing.

## What selection actually involves

When we source a stone for a specific elevation, the slabs are viewed and
numbered at origin against the drawing that will use them. Vein continuity is
mapped before anything ships, because once the pieces arrive the sequence is
fixed and any error is permanent.

That is not a procurement activity. It is a design activity that happens to take
place in a yard.

## The question worth asking

If you are considering a studio for a significant project, the useful question
is not which materials they can access. Nearly everyone can access nearly
everything now.

The question is who holds the drawing when the material is selected, and who is
accountable on site when it is installed. If the answer is one team, the
material has a chance of reading the way it was drawn. If it is three, the
outcome is decided by whoever happens to be on site that week.
